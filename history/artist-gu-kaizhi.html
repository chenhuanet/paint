顾恺之Gu Kaizhi

顾恺之是东晋画家、画论家，被后世称为“画绝”。他留下的原作几乎不存，《女史箴图》传为他的手笔，现存为唐代摹本。顾恺之主张“以形写神”，人物的大小、姿态、衣褶都要为眼神和气度服务；他画人物常常数年不点眼睛，因为眼睛是“神”的入口。Gu Kaizhi was a painter and theorist of the Eastern Jin, later called 'painting's supreme master'. Almost none of his original work survives; *Admonitions of the Instructive Lady* is attributed to him in a Tang dynasty copy. Gu argued for 'writing the spirit through form': scale, posture and drapery must serve the eyes and bearing. He often left the pupils unfinished for years, because the eye was the entrance to spirit.

小传Biography

《女史箴图》把张华的箴言拆成十二段，人物在绢上彼此顾盼，线细而不断。顾恺之的线不是轮廓，而是一种伦理的节奏：谁该低头，谁该侧身，谁的手该藏在袖子里，都在线里。中国人物画此后一千年的“传神”传统，几乎都从他这里出发。

Admonitions divides Zhang Hua's text into twelve scenes; figures glance at one another across the silk, and the line is thin but unbroken. Gu's line is not contour but an ethical rhythm: who lowers the head, who turns aside, whose hand hides in a sleeve. The next thousand years of Chinese figure painting's 'transmission of spirit' begins here.

先看眼睛和手：谁在看谁，谁的手在说话？Start with eyes and hands: who looks at whom, and whose hand is speaking?
