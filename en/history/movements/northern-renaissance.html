History · Movements

Northern Renaissance

1430–1570

Summary

Northern Renaissance painters did not work in Italian sunlight; they worked in the rain, stone walls, wool and ledgers of the Low Countries. Oil paint let them make the world transparent as glass: mirrors, windows, coins, fur, distant snow. Religious painting was not abandoned but filled with everyday detail; every object could be evidence of faith or a moral warning.

Traits

  • Detail as evidence: mirrors, notes, shoes, dogs and candles all take part in the narrative.
  • Transparent oil: layers of glaze give metal, fur and skin different reflections.
  • Everyday and moral: kitchen, snow, peasants and fools can all be parables.

Look

  • Zoom in for small things: why are they painted so precisely?
  • Read light: does it come from inside the picture rather than through a window?
  • Read space: is the room deep, or flattened by a mirror?
