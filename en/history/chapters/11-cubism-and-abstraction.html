Dismantling and Pure Colour: Cubism and Abstraction

1907–1930

Cubism: turning the object inside out

The core of Cubism is not 'painting like cubes' but admitting you cannot see an object from one angle. Gris's *Portrait of Picasso* puts front, profile and top view on one face; diagonals in the background interlock with the collar's triangles, and the portrait becomes a small building. Picasso's *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon* is more violent: five women's faces are replaced by African masks and geometric facets, and space folds like paper.

Analytical Cubism reduces colour to near-monochrome because colour interferes with 'reading structure'; Synthetic Cubism brings back colour, letters, newspaper and collage. Cubism turns painting from re-presentation into construction: the picture no longer points out the window but to its own parts.

Abstraction: retreat from the world, or distill it?

Kandinsky's *Composition VII* has no recognisable object but a strong sense of event: black lines explode, colour planes fragment, an invisible gravity holds the center. Kandinsky believed colour and sound could cross-sense; abstraction is the outward form of 'inner necessity'.

Malevich's *Black Square* reduces image to zero: a black square on white, like a world switched off and a new space switched on. Mondrian's *Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow* seeks balance with the fewest elements: red line, blue block, small yellow, black lines thicker and thinner. At the end of abstraction there is not emptiness but decision: which colour should weigh more, which line should be thicker.

Question

How is form broken, and how does the picture become pure colour and pure shape?

Thesis

After 1907 painting stopped pretending to be a window. Picasso and Braque broke objects into multiple viewpoints, Gris rebuilt the fragments as architecture, Delaunay set colour itself spinning, Kandinsky freed line from objects, Malevich reduced the world to a black square, Mondrian reduced the cosmos to horizontals, verticals and three primaries. Abstraction is not 'nothing'; it puts the grammar of painting on stage.

Look

  • Count the viewpoints: how many angles are present, and can they coexist?
  • Find gravity: which colour weighs most, and which line supports it?
  • Find the boundary: what survives in an abstract painting, and what has been deleted?
