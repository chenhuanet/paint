Mountains and Scrolls: Yuan, Ming and Qing Painting

1350–1728

Brushwork is a personality

*Dwelling in the Fuchun Mountains* is a scroll you can walk into: mountains unfold from right to left, banks, woods, fishing villages and mist appear in sequence. Huang Gongwang's brush does not map terrain; wet and dry, dark and light, fast and slow record how long a person walked in the mountains. Ni Zan is more extreme: the picture is almost empty, only a few trees, a pavilion, a stretch of water; blank silk matters more than ink.

Xu Wei's *Ink Grapes* turns brush into emotion: the grapes are not drawn one by one but splashed, dotted and swept; the poem beside them looks like another bunch. Shitao's Yellow Mountain refuses fixed contour; the mountain keeps changing inside ink dots. Literati 'personality' is not exaggerated expression but a brush method that cannot be copied.

Handscroll, inscription and collection

A handscroll is not one large picture but a portable stretch of time. The viewer unrolls an arm's length, stops to read inscriptions, look at seals, identify lineages. The painter's name may appear at the end; friends' comments, later collectors' poems and emperors' seals layer onto the same silk. The work is therefore not a 'finished product' but an archive continually rewritten.

Castiglione's *One Hundred Horses* reminds us that Qing court painting had another route: Western perspective, anatomy and chiaroscuro entered Chinese silk; horses gained volume, space gained depth. But it still obeyed court order: however free the herd, it is arranged inside the emperor's park.

Question

How did literati, brush and personality unfold in the handscroll?

Thesis

In Yuan, Ming and Qing painting, 'what' recedes and 'how' becomes the protagonist. Huang Gongwang uses hemp-fibre texture strokes for the earth of the Fuchun River, Ni Zan paints an empty pavilion and sparse trees with the least ink, Shen Zhou turns Mount Lu into a mountain you can travel through, Xu Wei turns grapes into a splash of ink anger, Shitao turns Yellow Mountain into changing ink dots. Literati painting is not an escape from society; it builds another society through brush, inscription, collecting and viewing.

Look

  • Slow down: read one section at a time, including inscriptions, seals and blank silk.
  • Read the brush: dry, wet, dark, light, fast, slow — which mark is the painter's fingerprint?
  • Read emptiness: is blank silk water, cloud, distance, or space refused to painting?
